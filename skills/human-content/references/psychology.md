# Persuasion and buyer psychology

Reference for picking the angle. Diagnose the reader's state first, then choose the one or two models that fit. Every model here is used honestly or not at all: fake scarcity, invented proof, and dark-pattern defaults destroy the trust they borrow against.

## Start by picking the right problem

- **Jobs to be done.** People hire products to get an outcome, not to own features. A drill buyer wants a hole. Frame everything around the job.
- **First principles / 5 whys.** Don't copy what competitors do; ask what problem the piece actually solves and whether content is even the answer.
- **Theory of constraints.** Fix the bottleneck. If traffic is low, conversion polish won't help; if the funnel leaks, more traffic won't either.
- **Local vs global optima.** Optimizing subject lines is pointless if email is the wrong channel. Zoom out before zooming in.
- **Inversion.** List what would guarantee the piece fails (confusing message, wrong audience, slow page), then prevent each.
- **Second-order thinking.** A flash sale lifts revenue now and trains customers to wait for discounts later. Check the effect of the effect.
- **80/20.** Find the 20% of channels, topics, and customers driving 80% of results; cut the rest.
- **Curse of knowledge.** Your product is obvious to you and confusing to newcomers. Test copy on people outside the space.

## How buyers think

- **Loss aversion.** Losses hurt about twice as much as equivalent gains please. "Don't lose your streak" beats "keep your streak going". Frame the cost of inaction.
- **Anchoring.** The first number seen shapes every later judgment. Show the higher reference first: original price, enterprise tier, cost of the status quo ("4 hours of rewriting" before "a 10-minute workflow").
- **Framing.** Same fact, different container. "90% success rate" vs "10% failure rate"; "$3/day" vs "$90/month"; "less than your morning coffee". Choose the frame that matches how the reader accounts for the cost.
- **Social proof / bandwagon.** Popularity signals safety. Customer counts, logos, reviews, "used by 200 agency owners". Specific and true, or omitted.
- **Authority.** Credentials, expert endorsements, "featured in". Defer-to-expert wiring.
- **Liking and unity.** People say yes to people like them. "Built by marketers for marketers", founder stories, insider language, shared identity.
- **Scarcity and urgency.** Limited availability raises perceived value. Only when genuine.
- **Mere exposure.** Familiarity breeds preference; roughly seven touches before a prospect converts. Consistent presence compounds.
- **Availability.** People judge likelihood by what comes easily to mind. Case studies and vivid testimonials make success feel reachable.
- **Confirmation bias.** Readers seek what confirms existing beliefs. Align with what the audience already believes and extend it; fighting beliefs head-on rarely works.
- **Status-quo bias and switching costs.** Change feels risky. Reduce it: "import your data in one click", migration guides, safe transitions.
- **Endowment and IKEA effects.** People overvalue what they own and what they helped build. Free trials create ownership; customization creates investment.
- **Zero-price effect.** Free is a category, not a price point. The jump from $1 to $0 is bigger than $2 to $1.
- **Present bias.** Immediate benefits beat future ones. "Start saving time today" beats "ROI in 6 months".
- **Regret aversion.** Fear of regretting the purchase blocks action. Guarantees, free trials, "cancel anytime".
- **Pratfall effect.** Admitting a small flaw makes the rest more credible. "We're not the cheapest, but..."
- **Peak-end rule.** Experiences are judged by their best moment and their ending. Design both.
- **Zeigarnik and goal-gradient effects.** Open loops nag; progress accelerates near the finish. "You're 80% done", progress bars, curiosity gaps that a click resolves.
- **Mimetic desire.** People want what others want. Waitlists and visible demand.

## Reducing friction

- **Behavior = motivation x ability x prompt.** All three or nothing happens. High motivation with a hard path fails; an easy path with no prompt fails. Design for all three.
- **Choice paralysis (Hick's law, paradox of choice).** More options, slower decisions, more abandonment. Three tiers beat seven; one CTA beats three; recommend a "best for most".
- **Defaults.** People accept the pre-selected option. Pre-select the plan you would recommend anyway.
- **Activation energy.** The first step decides everything. Pre-fill, template, show a quick win; make step one trivially easy.
- **Foot in the door.** Small commitment first (email, trial), then escalate. People stay consistent with what they have started.
- **EAST.** Make the behavior easy, attractive, social, timely.

## Pricing

- **Charm vs round prices.** $99 reads as value (the left digit dominates); $100 reads as premium. Match the ending to the positioning.
- **Rule of 100.** Under $100, percentage discounts feel bigger ("20% off $80"); over $100, absolute discounts do ("$100 off $500").
- **Good-better-best.** Three tiers with the target in the middle; the expensive tier is the anchor, the cheap tier the floor.
- **Decoy.** A clearly worse-value tier makes the target tier the obvious choice.
- **Contrast.** Show the "before" state vividly; the improvement is judged against it.

## Growth models

- **Compounding and flywheels.** Content, SEO, and brand build on themselves: content brings traffic brings customers brings case studies brings content. Start the loop; protect it.
- **Critical mass.** Win one segment deeply before widening. Depth before breadth.
- **Exploration vs exploitation.** Roughly 80% of effort on proven channels, 20% on experiments. Never abandon what works for what's shiny.
- **Survivorship bias.** The viral hit you're copying had 99 invisible failures. Study the failures too.
- **Cobra effect.** Incentives backfire: a referral bonus attracts gamers of the system. Test incentive structures.
- **Sunk cost.** Past spend never justifies future spend. Kill what isn't working.
- **Map vs territory.** Personas and dashboards model reality; talk to actual customers.

## Quick diagnosis

| The problem | Reach for |
|---|---|
| Low conversions | Choice paralysis, activation energy, motivation x ability x prompt |
| Price objections | Anchoring, framing, mental accounting, loss aversion |
| Trust deficit | Social proof, authority, reciprocity, pratfall |
| No urgency | Loss aversion, genuine scarcity, open loops |
| Churn | Endowment, switching costs, status-quo bias |
| Stalled growth | Theory of constraints, local vs global, compounding |
| Decision paralysis | Fewer options, defaults, a recommended tier |
| Weak onboarding | Goal gradient, IKEA effect, foot in the door |

Before shipping the angle, answer: what behavior is this influencing, what does the reader believe beforehand, where are they in the journey, and what is currently blocking the action?
