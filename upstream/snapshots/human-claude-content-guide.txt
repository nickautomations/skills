The problem is usually the setup
Generic AI writing is often a system problem. The prompt has no audience, voice samples, strategic goal, examples, copy rules or editing process. A useful content stack uses five capabilities in order: content strategy, marketing psychology, copywriting, humanizer and skill creator. The point is not to make Claude impersonate a person. It is to give the work a point of view and a repeatable quality bar.
Strategy Choose what to say for this audience and goal.
Psychology Choose why the angle should matter without manipulation.
Copy Improve hook, promise, proof, CTA and flow.
Humanizer Remove generic, inflated or robotic patterns.
Skill Creator Turn your voice and workflow into reusable instructions.

Source details: The brutal truth about "human-sounding" AI writing
A lot of people try to humanize AI text at the end.
That helps, but it is not enough.
If the idea is weak, Humanizer will only make a weak idea sound less robotic.
The real system is:
Strategy first.
Psychology second.
Copywriting third.
Humanizer fourth.
Custom voice last. That order matters.
If you start with Humanizer, you are polishing a draft before knowing whether the draft is worth publishing.
Do not do that.

Source details: What it helps remove
Use Humanizer to clean up:
1. Inflated language
Bad:
This groundbreaking system empowers users to unlock unprecedented productivity. Better:
This helps users get more done with less manual work. 2. Generic filler
Bad:
In today's fast-paced digital landscape, businesses must leverage innovative solutions. Better:
Most teams waste hours moving the same information between tools. 3. Forced contrasts
Bad:
It is not just a tool, it is a transformation. Better:
It works best when you use it for one repeated workflow. 4. Repetitive structure
Bad:
It saves time. It improves output. It increases productivity. It helps teams work smarter. Better:
It saves time by turning repeated work into a reusable workflow. 5. Robotic transitions
Bad:
Furthermore, it is important to note that... Better:
The important part is... 6. Overused AI phrases
Watch for phrases like:
unlock potential
seamless experience
cutting-edge
robust solution
game-changing
transformative
empowers users
dynamic landscape
These can be useful occasionally, but repeated together they make content sound machine-written.

Strategy before wording
Start by deciding the audience, problem, offer, content pillar and desired action. Then identify the psychological tension: what does the reader believe, fear, want or misunderstand? Only after that should the Copywriting pass strengthen the message. If the idea is weak, a Humanizer can only make weak thinking sound smoother.
Audience Who is this for and what do they already know?
Tension What desire, obstacle or belief makes the message relevant?
Proof What evidence or example supports the claim?
CTA What is the natural next action?
Source details: Quality checklist
Before publishing, ask:
Does it sound like a person wrote it?
Is there a clear point of view?
Did it remove vague AI phrasing?
Did it preserve meaning?
Did it avoid adding fake specificity?
Is the final version shorter and clearer?
If the answer is no, run another pass.

Source details: Useful principles to apply
Framing
How you present the same idea changes how it is perceived.
Example:
Instead of "AI helps you write faster," try "AI helps you stop starting from a blank page." Social proof
People trust what other people have already validated.
Example:
Used by 200 agency owners to repurpose sales calls into content. Only use this if it is true.
Anchoring
The first number, comparison, or reference point shapes judgment.
Example:
Instead of spending 4 hours rewriting a post, use this 10-minute editing workflow. Curiosity
A useful gap makes people want to continue.
Example:
Most AI writing sounds fake because it skips one step. Loss aversion
People often act faster to avoid a loss than to gain a benefit.
Example:
You are not just saving time. You are stopping generic content from weakening trust. Specificity
Specific claims are easier to believe than vague claims.
Bad:
Improve your content. Better:
Turn one rough idea into a hook, outline, caption, and CTA. Decision friction
People avoid action when the next step is unclear or feels risky.
Bad:
Get started now. Better:
Comment HUMAN and I will send you the setup guide.

Source details: Quality checklist
Before using a psychology-driven message, check:
Is the claim true?
Is the promise believable?
Is the audience respected?
Is the principle relevant?
Is the message clearer, not just more persuasive?
Would I stand behind this publicly?
If not, do not use it.
Humanizer as a final pass
Use a Humanizer when the draft is inflated, vague, repetitive, overly polished or full of generic AI phrasing. Ask it to preserve meaning, remove filler, add specificity only from supplied facts and flag unsupported claims. Do not use it to invent stories, personality or proof. The strongest voice comes from your examples, decisions and point of view.
Remove Inflated language, filler, repetitive transitions and vague claims.
Preserve Facts, meaning, examples and the author's real voice.
Flag Claims that need evidence rather than smoothing them over.

Source details: Custom voice SKILL.md starter
Use this if you want a simple manual version.
# My Voice Content Skill
## Description
Use this Skill whenever I ask Claude to write, rewrite, edit, or improve content in my voice for social posts, scripts, captions, carousels, newsletters, or founder-led content.
## Voice
Write in a direct, practical, human style.
The voice should be:
- clear
- sharp
- simple
- useful
- specific
- strategic
- founder-focused
Avoid:
- generic AI language
- hype
- corporate jargon
- long paragraphs
- over-polished phrasing
- fake urgency
- unsupported claims
## Audience
The audience includes creators, founders, marketers, agency owners, operators, and AI-curious professionals who want practical systems.
## Structure
For educational content:
1. Start with a strong claim or useful tension.
2. Name the common mistake.
3. Explain the better system.
4. Give examples.
5. End with a clear action or takeaway.
## Editing rules
When editing:
1. Cut filler.
2. Make the point sharper.
3. Preserve meaning.
4. Keep the tone human.
5. Make the CTA clearer.
6. Remove repeated phrasing.
7. Do not add facts that were not provided.
## Output
When asked to create content, provide:
- best hook
- structured draft
- caption if needed
- CTA
- 3 alternate hooks
- final quality check
Source details: Quality checklist
Your custom voice Skill is working if:
outputs sound closer to you
Claude avoids your banned phrases
drafts need fewer rewrites
structure becomes more consistent
hooks become sharper
CTAs become clearer
your examples influence the output
the Skill activates only when relevant
If it makes every post sound the same, the Skill is too rigid.
If it barely changes anything, the instructions are too vague.
Create your own content Skill
Once you have examples you genuinely like, extract the patterns: sentence length, vocabulary, structure, rhythm, formatting, hooks, transitions and things you never say. Package those rules with examples, templates and a quality checklist. A Skill should make the next draft easier to start and easier to review. It should not become a rigid imitation of old work.
Collect Five to fifteen strong examples and why each works.
Extract Voice, structure, formatting, audience and quality rules.
Test Run a new topic through the Skill and revise the instructions.
